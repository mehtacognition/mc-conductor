# Conductor Onboarding

Conductor works best when every skill can draw from the same small profile of the leader, organization, sources, and cadence. Do this once before installing the full bundle.

The goal is not to build a personality quiz or a management dossier. The goal is to give the skills enough context to adapt their questions, examples, posture, and output to the leader's real work.

## The Three Setup Layers

### 1. Conductor Profile

Stable context about the leader and their organization.

### 2. Source Map

The sources the leader can realistically bring into the cadence.

### 3. Cadence Contract

The rituals the leader will actually run, when, and with what input.

Do not install all eleven skills and hope a rhythm appears. Start with the smallest cadence the leader will sustain.

## Setup Interview

Ask these questions in one conversation. Short answers are fine.

### Role And Context

1. What is your role, and what decisions are uniquely yours?
2. What kind of organization, institution, company, or community are you leading?
3. Who are your five most important stakeholder groups?
4. What is the strategic arc you are in right now: growth, turnaround, stabilization, transition, integration, succession, crisis, reinvention, or something else?
5. What are the 2-3 questions currently taking up the most leadership oxygen?

### Leadership Patterns

6. What does your calendar tend to reward that your strategy says should matter less?
7. What do people most often misunderstand about how you lead?
8. Where do you tend to over-function: rescuing, deciding too much, avoiding conflict, over-explaining, chasing consensus, absorbing ambiguity, or something else?
9. How direct should Conductor be with you: gentle, direct, or very direct?
10. What should Conductor never do with you: invent facts, sound therapeutic, become a task manager, overstep legal/personnel boundaries, soften too much, push too hard, or something else?

### Communication Duties

11. What kinds of communication do you regularly have to produce: board notes, investor updates, faculty or staff messages, community newsletters, speeches, team memos, customer updates, donor notes, public essays, or something else?
12. Which audiences need the most careful framing from you right now?
13. What words, phrases, or tones should Conductor avoid because they do not sound like you?

### Source Map

14. What sources can you realistically bring into a daily or weekly rhythm?
15. Which sources are available only occasionally?
16. Which sources are sensitive or off-limits?
17. Where do your commitments usually live: calendar, email, assistant notes, CRM, project tracker, Slack or Teams, board portal, paper notebook, journal, or elsewhere?
18. Where do your best stories and examples usually live: meeting notes, classroom or customer visits, speeches, community messages, newsletters, transcripts, journal notes, or elsewhere?

### Cadence Contract

19. Which cadence will you start with for the first two weeks?
20. When will you run it?
21. What input will you bring each time?
22. What would make this useful enough that you keep using it?

## Optional Profile Deepening: Exec Review

After the base Conductor Profile is complete, run **Exec Review** when the leader has an assistant, chief of staff, executive team, board workflow, or heavy document-review load. Exec Review is not another cadence ritual. It is a profile-deepening tool that helps others manage up to the leader by understanding how the leader reviews, decides, pushes back, and communicates.

Use the generated **Leader Review Profile** as a companion to the Conductor Profile. It should sharpen the shared profile with decision criteria, repeated feedback questions, green-light signals, pushback triggers, communication preferences, document-review checklists, stress signals, and validated vs. inferred review comments.

## Recommended Starting Paths

### For A New Executive Or Institutional Leader

Start with:

1. Sunday Reflection
2. Morning Brief
3. Weekly 1:1
4. Coaching Diagnostic when a specific issue feels stuck

Why: new leaders need rhythm, stakeholder listening, and fast pattern recognition before adding more output.

### For A Founder Or CEO In Growth Mode

Start with:

1. Morning Brief
2. Evening Hot Take
3. Friday Pattern Read
4. Leader-Pen

Why: the calendar moves quickly, and strategic framing needs to be captured before it disappears into execution.

### For A Nonprofit Or Community Leader

Start with:

1. Sunday Reflection
2. Monthly Review
3. Leader-Pen
4. Coaching Diagnostic

Why: community leadership often turns on narrative, trust, board alignment, and repeated stakeholder patterns.

### For A Board Chair Or Governance Leader

Start with:

1. Coaching Diagnostic
2. Monthly Review
3. Quarterly Positioning
4. Leader-Edit

Why: the highest-value work is often framing, decision ownership, and distinguishing governance signal from operational noise.

### For A Leader With A Strong Assistant Or Chief Of Staff

Start with:

1. Morning Brief
2. Exec Review
3. Friday Pattern Read
4. Leader-Edit

Why: the leverage comes from making prep, document review, managing up, and open-loop surfacing repeatable outside the leader's head.

## How Skills Should Use The Profile

Every Conductor skill should follow this rule:

If a Conductor Profile is available, use it to adapt the output. Do not re-ask setup questions already answered in the profile.

If no Conductor Profile is available, ask only the minimum context needed for the current run. Do not force the full onboarding interview unless the user is intentionally setting up Conductor.

Use the profile to adapt:

- Which stakeholders matter.
- Which source gaps are meaningful.
- How direct to be.
- Which leadership patterns to watch.
- Which communication formats matter.
- Which examples and strategic-framing candidates are useful.

Do not use the profile to stereotype the leader or overfit every output. The day's evidence still matters more than the profile.

## Maintenance

Update the Conductor Profile:

- After a role change.
- At the start of a new strategic arc.
- After a quarterly review.
- When the leader's source stack changes.
- When the leader says, "This does not sound like me."

Small updates are better than a full re-onboarding. The profile should stay useful, not become another artifact nobody trusts.
