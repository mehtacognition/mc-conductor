# Conductor - Sunday Reflection

Part of the **MC Conductor** bundle. Friday notices. Sunday makes meaning.

The Sunday Reflection asks: **Who are you becoming through the way you are leading, and is that who you want to be?**

It is the weekly metacognitive ritual. It should be more reflective than the Friday Pattern Read, but still grounded in evidence.

## Conductor Profile

If a Conductor Profile is available, use it to adapt this skill to the leader's role, organization, stakeholders, strategic arc, source map, communication duties, directness preference, and off-limits areas.

If no profile is available, ask only the minimum context needed for this run. Do not force the full onboarding interview unless the user is intentionally setting up Conductor. See `docs/onboarding.md` for the shared setup flow.

## Cadence rule

If the leader also runs **Monthly Review** on the first Sunday of each month, the Monthly Review should subsume Sunday Reflection that week. Do not generate two overlapping reflections unless the leader explicitly asks for both.

## What to bring

Bring your own sources:

1. This week's Friday Pattern Read.
2. Morning Briefs and Evening Hot Takes.
3. Calendar/time audit for the week.
4. Journal notes, voice notes, or personal reflection.
5. 1:1 notes, team notes, board notes, customer/client notes.
6. Previous Sunday Reflection, Monthly Review, Quarterly Positioning, or Coaching Diagnostic Brief.
7. Next week's calendar.

If the Friday Pattern Read exists, start there. Friday provides the week's pattern synthesis. Sunday adds the metacognitive layer. Do not reconstruct what Friday already noticed unless Friday is missing or thin.

## Evidence protocol

Track the source state:

- **Used:** source informed the reflection.
- **Provided, no signal:** source was available but did not change the read.
- **Missing:** source would materially improve confidence.

End with a short `Sources used:` line. Do not claim source coverage you do not have.

## What Sunday is responsible for

Sunday turns evidence into meaning:

- What became clearer?
- What became muddier?
- Where did the leader's calendar reflect or betray stated priorities?
- What created energy?
- What drained it?
- What identity is the leader practicing?
- Which relationships need attention?
- What should matter most next week?

## Step 1: Establish the week

Define the week under review. If next week's calendar is provided, use it only for the "Setting Up Next Week" section.

## Step 2: Gather cascade input

Start with:

- Friday Pattern Read as primary input.
- This week's Morning Briefs and Evening Hot Takes as supporting input.
- Prior Sunday Reflection for stated priorities.

Supplement with raw sources only when they sharpen or challenge the cascade.

## Step 3: Accountability data pull

Before answering the five questions, compare stated priorities against lived time:

- What did last Sunday's reflection or Monday's brief say mattered?
- What did the calendar and actual work show?
- Did protected time survive?
- Did the leader's attention match stated strategic importance?

If the gap is large, name it directly. Do not hide drift inside polite phrasing.

## The five questions

### 1. Strategic Clarity

What's becoming clearer about where you are headed, and what's getting muddier?

Draw from Friday's themes, decisions, and accountability gaps. If the calendar shows drift from stated priorities, ask whether it was deliberate pivot or passive drift.

### 2. Intellectual Development

What are you learning from customers, clients, board members, investors, direct reports, reading, writing, the market, or the work itself?

This is not "lessons learned" theater. It is the leader's thinking edge.

### 3. Energy Patterns

Where did energy come from this week, and where did it go?

Track meetings, work types, relationships, decisions, travel, writing, deep work, and unresolved tension. Energy is evidence.

### 4. Identity Evolution

Who are you becoming through this work, and is that who you want to be?

Look for the gap between stated identity and lived behavior:

- Builder vs. rescuer.
- Strategist vs. responder.
- Decider vs. consensus-seeker.
- Coach vs. answer machine.
- Executive vs. chief problem absorber.

### 5. Relational Influence

How are you showing up for the people who matter, and how are they showing up for you?

Do not turn this into a CRM report. Name the relationships where attention, trust, tension, dependence, or distance changed.

## Step 4: Cross-question synthesis

After the five questions, identify:

- Through-line: what connects the answers.
- Tension: where the answers conflict.
- Actionable clarity: what is becoming clear enough to act on.

Then surface **one forcing question** based on the evidence. Choose the most fitting:

- If stated priorities and actual time diverged: "What decision are you currently delaying that you already know the answer to?"
- If all addition and no subtraction: "What have you explicitly stopped doing this month? Not paused. Stopped."
- If protected time keeps getting consumed: "What's the most uncomfortable truth about how you're spending your time that you've softened when describing it to yourself?"
- If relational drift is visible: "Who is experiencing your absence as ambiguity?"
- If the leader keeps rescuing: "What problem did you solve this week that someone else needed to own?"

Choose one. The question is the intervention.

## Step 5: Set up next week

Use next week's calendar and the reflection to set up Monday morning:

- Week ahead at a glance.
- Three things that matter most next week.
- One thing to stop, decline, defer, or protect.
- One conversation to prepare before it becomes urgent.

Keep this section tight. These are inputs to the next week, not another reflection.

## Output format

```
## Sunday Reflection - Week of [Date]

### Strategic Clarity
[2-4 paragraphs.]

### Intellectual Development
[2-4 paragraphs.]

### Energy Patterns
[2-4 paragraphs.]

### Identity Evolution
[2-4 paragraphs.]

### Relational Influence
[2-4 paragraphs.]

### Synthesis
[Through-line, tension, and one forcing question.]

### Setting Up Next Week
[Week shape, three priorities, one subtraction/protection, one conversation.]

Sources used: [Used sources; missing sources that materially limit confidence.]
```

## Quality bar

- 1,500-2,500 words is the normal range.
- Reflective, not therapeutic.
- Metacognitive, not reportorial.
- Direct, candid, curious.
- Evidence-grounded, not vibes.
- No generic gratitude journaling.
- No tidy moral at the end.
- If there is drift between priority and calendar, say so.
- If the leader is becoming the bottleneck, say so.

## Where this fits in the Conductor rhythm

The Sunday Reflection uses the **Friday Pattern Read** as primary input. It sets the frame for the next week's **Morning Briefs** and gives the **Monthly Review** its weekly evidence.
